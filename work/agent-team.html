Running a team of AI agents

I run about twenty Claude Code sessions as one team. A coordinator splits up the work, each project has a lead and builders, and anything that needs money, an account or my name comes to me first.

01Problem

One AI session is easy. Twenty at once step on each other: two edit the same file, one decides something only I should decide, another says "done" without checking. I wanted a setup where many agents can work in parallel and I can still trust what comes back.

02What I built

  • A coordinator that hands out work and keeps track of who is doing what.
  • A small team per project: a lead that plans and reviews, and builders that do the work.
  • Shared notes, so a new session can pick up where another one stopped.
  • One rule above the rest: anything involving money, accounts, publishing or contacting a person waits for me.
  • Helper models from other companies for research and second opinions, checked before anything they say is used.

03Results

  • 35 trading-model variants run and checked in under a week (see Market Impact Lab).
  • Decisions that need me arrive as short written questions with a recommended answer, so the agents don't guess and don't stall.
  • One real failure: a test touched one of my real apps and a key ended up in a local log. It was caught the same day, I replaced the key, and tests now only use windows they create themselves.

04What I learned

  • Agents are fast and confident. The checks around them are what make them trustworthy.
  • Written notes beat chat: work survives when a session ends.
  • Rules get written after something goes wrong.
